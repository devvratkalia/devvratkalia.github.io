Your textbook, paced to the exam

Syllabook gives every chapter of your book its days before the exam, opens the book at the right pages when it is time, and has you check what you remember once the book is closed.

  • App StoreiPhone, iPad and Mac
  • Google PlayAndroid phones and tablets

The first 3 days include everything, and one subject stays free after them. Syllabook used to be called StudyMaster.

One loop for every topic

Book, pace, block, check. Each topic in your book goes round it until the exam.

  1. Book

    Photograph the contents pages, choose the PDF, or type the topics. Chapters, topics and printed page numbers all come along.

  2. Pace

    Give each exam its day, start time, chapters and revision days. Every topic gets a day before it, and so does every review.

  3. Block

    Forty minutes with the book open at a new topic's pages. Twenty for a review, five for a check. A break follows a new topic, or forty minutes of study in a row.

  4. Check

    Close the book. Say, type or think through what you remember, tick your own key points, and pick the grade yourself.

Around the loop

Four tabs hold everything: Today, Subjects, Exams and Settings.

Today

The day's timetable, with the pages for every block. It stays put once the day begins, and a quiet line keeps your streak.

Subjects

A shelf with a book for each subject. Every topic shows where it stands, in a mark and a word.

  • To start
  • Learning
  • Secure
  • Shaky

Exams

Your exam dates in a row, each marked On track, Tight or At risk from counts and dates. Up to two changes are offered, each previewed before you apply it.

Errata

Every point you miss, kept with its page. The ones you were sure of come back before the block ends, and each clears after two later days of getting it right.

The last days

No new topics in an exam's final week, Errata first, and a 10-minute Last look on the evening before.

Recaps and notes

Record a topic in your own words and revise it by ear later. Keep typed or handwritten notes, even on a PDF's pages.

Questions from your pages

A check is only as good as what you test yourself on. You can type or paste key points, tap sentences in the book to keep them, turn a sentence into a question, start from a question starter, or let your points become fill-ins.

On an iPhone, iPad or Mac that supports Apple Intelligence, Syllabook can also set up to three questions from a page you choose. Each one asks what a term means or how something works, and quotes the sentence on that page that answers it. Nothing is kept until you tap Keep, a kept question carries its page and a label saying how it was made, and Report this question lets you tell us when one is wrong.

Needs iOS, iPadOS or macOS 26 or later with Apple Intelligence on, and a book in English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese or Chinese. The questions are set by Apple's model on your own device. You can switch them off in Settings › Studying.

Free days, and one free subject

Your first 3 days include everything. After that, one subject keeps its whole plan without paying. Your other subjects stay on the shelf with a clasp: you can still open them, export them and back them up, and a membership plans them again.

A membership is paid monthly or yearly, or once with nothing to renew. The monthly and yearly plans begin with a free week if you have not had one. Your store shows the price for your country before you confirm.

Bought on the App Store, it covers iPhone, iPad and Mac. Bought on Google Play, it covers Android.

Questions and answers

If yours isn't here, write to us: the address is at the foot of the page.

How do I add my book?

When you add a subject, choose how its book comes in: photograph its contents pages, choose its PDF, or type the topics one to a line. A PDF's bookmarks or its contents page become the topics. Syllabook keeps the chapters, the topics and the page numbers printed in the book. With a PDF, each topic opens at its own pages; with a paper book, each block tells you which page to turn to. A PDF can also be added later.

How does Syllabook choose what I study each day?

Topics start in the book's order and are spread over the days before their exam, leaving the revision days you asked for. After a check, a topic comes back after a gap that depends on your grade, and the gap is always cut short so its last review falls before the exam. The day's timetable is set when the day starts, so it doesn't move about while you study.

What happens during a block?

You read with the book open at the topic's pages: 40 minutes for something new, 20 for a review, 5 for a check. Then you close the book and say, type or think through what you remember, note how sure you were, and tick the key points you had. A typed answer ticks the points it matches, and you can change any tick. Each grade, Perfect, Good, Hard or Forgot, shows the date it leads to, and you choose. A break follows, a quarter as long as the block.

What is Errata?

The list of points you missed in checks, each with the page it comes from. Points you were sure of and still missed come back at the end of the same block. A point leaves the list once you have it right on two later days. In an exam's last week Errata come first, and on the evening before, a 10-minute Last look goes over them.

What does the Exams tab show?

Your exam dates in order, then a card for each exam with plain counts: how many topics are secure now, and how many could be by exam day. Each exam is marked On track, Tight or At risk. When an exam is tight, Syllabook can offer up to two changes, such as an hour more a day, and shows what each does before you apply it. Readiness is shown in counts, never as a percentage.

Which devices can set questions from my pages?

An iPhone, iPad or Mac that supports Apple Intelligence, on iOS, iPadOS or macOS 26 or later, with Apple Intelligence on, for a book in English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese or Chinese. The book's language decides, whatever language the app is in. On Android, on earlier systems and for other languages, the same places offer a question from a sentence, question starters, fill-ins and questions you write yourself.

What if a question set from a page is wrong?

Cross it out, or edit it before you keep it. To tell us, open the question's More menu and choose Report this question. You see exactly what the email will hold: the question, its answer, the quoted sentence, the page and how it was made. It opens in your own mail app, and nothing goes anywhere unless you send it.

Where are my books, PDFs and recordings kept?

On your device, in Syllabook's own storage: your PDFs, notes, voice recaps, recorded checks and questions included. We run no server, so there is nothing of yours for us to see. Settings › Backups saves everything to one file, with or without the PDFs, for safekeeping or for moving to another device.

What happens to my subjects after the first 3 days?

Your first subject keeps its whole plan for free. The others keep a clasp on the shelf: you can open them, read their notes, export them and back them up, and starting one shows what a membership adds. Nothing you have added is deleted or hidden.

How do I restore or cancel a membership?

Syllabook has no account; it asks the App Store or Google Play what you have bought. On a new device, sign in to the same store and choose Restore Purchases at the foot of the membership offer. To cancel on an iPhone, iPad or Mac, open your Apple Account settings, then Subscriptions, at least 24 hours before the current period ends. On Android, open Google Play, then Payments & subscriptions, then Subscriptions. A one-time payment has nothing to cancel.

I used StudyMaster. What changes?

StudyMaster is now called Syllabook, with a new design built around your book and your exams. When you update, your subjects, topics, notes, recordings and history come across on your device, and a membership you already pay for carries on as it is. Insights and the readiness percentages have made way for the Exams tab, which uses counts and dates.

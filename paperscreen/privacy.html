PaperScreen Privacy Policy

App: PaperScreen (com.devvratkalia.paperScreen) · Publisher: Dev_K
Effective date: 9 August 2026 · Contact: wekstuff@gmail.com

PaperScreen turns your display into a paper-like, low-glare surface and reminds you to rest your eyes (the 20-20-20 rule) and to stand up during long sessions. It runs on Android, iOS, iPadOS and macOS.

This policy explains, in plain English, what leaves your device and what does not. It applies to every platform we ship on.

The short version

  • We do not ask you to create an account. We never see your name, email or contacts.
  • Your settings — screen profiles, colours, reminder intervals, schedules — stay on your device.
  • PaperScreen does not read, record or transmit what is on your screen. The paper effect is a display filter, not a screen capture.
  • The one thing that does leave your device is advertising data, collected by our ads SDK. That is described in detail below, and you can limit or switch off personalised ads.
  • Buying PaperScreen Pro removes ads, and with them the advertising data collection.

1. Who is responsible for your data

PaperScreen is published by Dev_K. For anything in this policy — questions, requests, complaints — write to wekstuff@gmail.com. Under the EU/UK GDPR, Dev_K is the data controller for the advertising data described in section 3.

2. What stays on your device

The following never leaves your device and is never sent to us:

  • Your screen profiles — paper tints, warmth, contrast, texture, grain and any custom profiles you create in Pro.
  • Your reminder settings — 20-20-20 timing, stand-up interval, quiet hours, per-app or per-schedule rules, snooze behaviour.
  • Your reminder history — counts of breaks taken or skipped, used only to draw your own in-app streaks and stats.
  • Launch-at-startup and background-service preferences.

This data is stored in the app's own private storage on your device. If your device backs itself up (iCloud, Google Backup), the backup is handled by Apple or Google under their terms, not by us. Deleting the app deletes this data.

PaperScreen does not capture your screen

On every platform, the paper effect is applied as an overlay or a display-colour adjustment. The app does not use screen recording, screenshots, accessibility content reading, keystroke logging or clipboard access to learn what you are working on. We have no way to see your code, documents, browsing or reading material, and we do not want it.

3. What we collect: advertising data

The free version of PaperScreen shows ads through Unity LevelPlay mediation with the Unity Ads adapter (banner, interstitial and rewarded formats). Mediation software collects device and advertising identifiers as soon as it initialises — that is, whether or not you ever see or tap an ad.

What is collected Why Shared with third parties? Used to track you across apps?
Advertising identifier (Google Advertising ID on Android, IDFA on Apple platforms) Serving and measuring ads; frequency capping Yes — the mediated ad networks Yes
App-set ID / vendor ID (a resettable identifier scoped to the app) Advertising; fraud and invalid-traffic prevention Yes Yes
Ad interaction events (impressions, clicks, rewarded-ad completions) Advertising and ad analytics Yes Yes
Coarse device and app information (device model, OS version, language and locale, network type, app version) Ad delivery and compatibility; analytics Yes Yes
Diagnostics — crash logs and performance data from the ads SDK Keeping the app and SDK working Yes — the SDK vendor No

None of this is linked to a personal identity, because PaperScreen has no accounts and never sends your name, email or a user ID alongside it.

What we do not collect: we do not collect your precise or approximate location, contacts, photos, files, health data, screen content, browsing history, or any content you create outside the app. PaperScreen has no analytics SDK of its own, and no AI features that send text anywhere.

Who receives it

  • Unity Technologies — operator of Unity LevelPlay mediation (formerly ironSource) and the Unity Ads network. Their privacy policy: unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy
  • Mediated demand partners — the ad networks LevelPlay routes an impression to. Each processes the identifiers above under its own policy. The current list is available from us on request at wekstuff@gmail.com.
  • Google Play services (Android) — supplies the advertising ID and app-set ID.
  • Apple (iOS, iPadOS, macOS) — supplies the IDFA, and only after you allow tracking (see below).

4. Purchases and subscriptions

PaperScreen Pro is sold as an auto-renewing subscription (pro_monthly and pro_yearly) through the Apple App Store or Google Play. The store handles the entire transaction.

  • We never see or receive your card number, billing address or payment details.
  • The app receives an anonymous purchase receipt from the store so it can unlock Pro features. It contains no personal information about you.
  • Your subscription status is validated against the store and cached on your device.
  • Apple and Google's own privacy policies govern the purchase itself.

Once Pro is active, ads are switched off and the advertising data collection in section 3 stops.

5. Permissions and background operation

PaperScreen asks for the minimum it needs. Each permission is used only for the feature named.

  • Notifications — to deliver 20-20-20 eye-rest prompts and stand-up reminders. Reminder content is generated on your device and never sent through a server; there is no push infrastructure and no remote messaging.
  • Display over other apps / screen overlay (Android) — to draw the paper filter above whatever you are using.
  • Run at startup and background execution — so the paper filter and your reminder schedule survive a reboot and keep working while you are in other apps. The background service only applies your filter and runs your timers. It does not scan, log or upload anything about your activity.
  • Battery-optimisation exemption (Android, optional) — reminders on some devices are unreliable without it. You can decline and the app still works.
  • Menu-bar item and Screen Recording permission (macOS) — some macOS display-adjustment techniques require the Screen Recording permission at the system level. If you would rather not grant it, the app falls back to its colour-profile mode.
  • App Tracking Transparency (Apple platforms) — the prompt shown before any advertising identifier is read.
